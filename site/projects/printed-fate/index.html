01 / The idea

What do we bring
to a question of fate?

Printed Fate grew from my interest in how people use rituals to navigate uncertainty. Drawing on Wuxing, Bagua, and the I Ching, I explored a contemporary experience around an ancient practice: asking a question, casting coins, and interpreting a response.

The project invites curiosity about choice and destiny. An AI conversation becomes one part of that experience, alongside the pace of the ritual, the atmosphere of the room, and an object to keep.

02 / The interaction

One ritual.
Two ways to enter.

I designed a physical installation and a web experience around a shared sequence. Visitors bring a question, cast three coins six times, and receive an interpretation connected to the I Ching’s 64 hexagrams.

Experience framework / physical and digital pathways

The digital layer

Give the conversation
a sense of ceremony.

The interface begins with a quiet invitation to pause and formulate a question. A conversational guide then accompanies the coin-casting sequence and reading. The interaction unfolds through a series of steps, giving each moment its own space.

Connecting the dialogue

I prototyped the conversational flow in Voiceflow and used the OpenAI Assistants API for the AI layer. The flow links the visitor’s question and coin-casting sequence to a generated interpretation.

The surrounding interaction gives the generated text a context: an invitation, an action, a response, and a moment to reflect.

03 / The making

From a digital reading
to a physical presence.

I developed the installation through sketches, 3D modeling, laser-cut layers, and assembly tests. Three coin forms sit within a flowing silhouette, bringing the central gesture of the ritual into the object itself.

04 / The experience

Let the result
leave the screen.

TouchDesigner visuals and DMX lighting extend the experience into the room. The physical journey concludes with a reading on paper, placed in a glass bottle that can be opened immediately or saved for later.

Reflection

Designing the space around AI.

Printed Fate brings conversational design, visual programming, and fabrication into one experience. The AI response sits within a larger sequence of choices: what someone asks, how they participate, and when they choose to open the result.

The project explores how ritual and physical form can invite personal reflection. Its outcome is an interactive prototype and installation, with room to further study how people interpret and return to their readings.
