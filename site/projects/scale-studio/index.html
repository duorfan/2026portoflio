01 / Core product workflows

Help teams understand
what needs their attention.

Scale Studio connects customer-created content with the brand teams who review, organize, and use it. I designed the workflows that turn that activity into a clear next step.

Progress needs a source of truth.

An early campaign card compared collected assets with a contract guarantee. But campaigns had no target field to supply that goal. I removed goal-pacing from the campaign card, kept actual counts and trends, and moved contract-level progress to the Dashboard.

Asset Library / Interaction detail

Search should find
the tag you remember.

A usability session exposed whole-word-only matching. Restoring a separate Tags filter would add another control without fixing the search behavior.

I specified case-insensitive contains matching, with suggestions from two characters, across searchable lists. “box” can find Box set, Gift box, Toybox, and Unboxing.

02 / Aurora & engineering handoff

A design system that
survives the handoff.

I translated Aurora’s visual direction into shared tokens and component rules. The intent was to make consistency part of the build, rather than something to repair screen by screen.

A token-bound base gradient with aurora layers. The foundation stays consistent while the composition can vary.

02

Two tiers of glass

Pulse glass for hero and insight surfaces. Frost glass for utility cards and dense work areas.

03

Shared geometry, semantic variants, and defined focus states carry the interaction across screens.

03 / Analytics that explain quality

Make the mix of content
easy to interpret.

A chart should make the relationship between values clear. I redesigned the quality distribution to show what each category contributes to the whole.

One denominator.
A more honest picture.

Individually normalized columns made a partial share look full-height. I chose a single 100% segmented bar with a legend, so all categories use the same denominator.

The decision became a reusable component in Aurora, making the same visual logic available across the product.

Another quality decision: giving AI the right context ＋

The rubric was judging the wrong environment.

A grocery client’s capture flow was rejecting roughly 90% of submissions. The setup was incomplete, and the rubric assumed restaurant-style content. Rather than lower the bar or retrain users, I wrote grocery-appropriate guidance: busy shelves and national brands can be valid content.

I also identified that scoring ignored the moment tag sent by the app and filed an engineering issue. After the configuration change, a dinner photo passed under Delivery—one observed result, not a measured improvement in the overall pass rate.

What I take forward

Clarity takes judgment,
even when execution gets faster.

I cut invented auth screens from a visual refresh and narrowed a capture-tips fix to two changes inside the existing card. Across the work, the same principle held: resolve the actual problem, encode the decision, then use AI to move faster.
