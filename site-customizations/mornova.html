01 / The experience

Mornings change.
An alarm should notice.

During graduate school, my schedule kept changing, but my alarm treated every morning the same. I wanted a wake-up experience that could respond to the day ahead and still feel calm.

Mornova brings together calendar and weather context, an AI conversation, and ambient light. The design question became: how can a morning assistant adapt while leaving the person in control?

02 / Three design decisions

Personal in tone.
Practical in use.

I treated personalization as a set of interaction choices: how Mornova speaks, what context it uses, and how someone can respond without reaching for a screen.

01

Let people choose
how they’re addressed.

A helpful message can still feel wrong in the wrong voice. Calm, Direct, Playful, and Friendly personalities let people choose the tone of their morning companion.

CalmDirectPlayfulFriendly
02

Use context.
Preserve a way to adjust.

Weather and calendar information give the assistant a reason to adapt. Wake-time settings, routine controls, and snooze keep the experience open to the person’s own judgment.

Context → Suggestion → Choice

A recommendation should support the routine.

03

Give the interaction
a physical home.

Light carries part of the experience into the room. A pressure sensor provides a direct snooze gesture: press and hold for five seconds, without navigating the dashboard.

03 / Making the physical product

The geometry had
to work in the workshop.

The lamp combines a printed core, a layered acrylic stand, and an engraved acrylic light sheet. Building it exposed a problem that the CAD model alone had not resolved: how to print, assemble, and route the electronics cleanly.

A clean exterior.
A difficult print.

Vertical hollows and overhangs needed dense internal supports. Removing them was slow and left some areas rough or fragile. The structure also needed more deliberate space for wiring.

Change the shape.
Simplify the build.

Tapered, leaf-shaped hollows could bridge during printing without supports. I refined wire channels and alignment with the acrylic parts, making assembly cleaner and carrying the organic motif through the object.

The lesson

The shape became more coherent when I designed around how it would actually be made.

05 / Outcome & reflection

Built end to end.
Still learning from it.

The physical lamp, AI conversation, and live calendar and weather connections worked together. I tested the system myself and with friends.

Testing so far has been informal. The next step is a structured study of how people understand and use Mornova in their everyday routines.

What I take forward

The interaction cannot be designed only on a screen. Sensor placement, the feel of a gesture, and the way light moves through the material all shape the experience. Iterating on the object made those decisions tangible.

What I would validate next

A structured study of setup, morning use, and snooze: can someone understand the recommendation, adjust it confidently, and use the lamp without assistance? Repeated use would help distinguish a pleasing first impression from a useful routine.
